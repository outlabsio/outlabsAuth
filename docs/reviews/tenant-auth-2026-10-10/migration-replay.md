# Independent migration, replay, history and recovery review

**Verdict: revise the narrow replay/delivery/recovery contracts before accepting those guarantees.** The independent namespace and staged compatibility direction is coherent in this lane. The proposal correctly treats genuine upgrade proof, legacy conversion, all-flow qualification, persistent grants, host reconciliation and deployment as separate gates. I found one high and three medium issues at the intersections of those contracts. None is a claim of an exploit in the released library or of a completed tenant implementation.

## New findings

### F-MR-01: database restoration can rewind the very fences that must survive recovery

- **Severity:** High.
- **Evidence category:** proposal / recovery acceptance gap; design counterexample.
- **Revision / lane:** revision 0.1 at the exact commit above; replay and recovery.
- **Requirement / use case:** R13, R20–R24; U14, U34, U42–U44.
- **Decision dependency:** D10, D17, D20; the recovery-generation/high-water-mark rule is not explicitly registered.
- **Claim and evidence:** `docs/TENANT_IDENTITIES_AND_ADMINISTRATIVE_GRANTS.md:1297–1298` requires a newer reviewed watermark to fence stale plans and forbids old-source authority after cutover. Lines `1307–1311` require a durable monotonic runner epoch. Lines `1756–1766` permit coordinated database restoration and require stale source replays to remain fenced, but do not specify a restore-resistant authority generation or a mandatory reconstruction boundary before admission. Restoring all participating schemas together preserves consistency at the backup time; it also restores their earlier fencing epochs, watermarks, cutover state and revocation markers. Current revocation is demonstrably database state: `outlabs_auth/models/sql/token.py:93–104,145–149`; current refresh admission checks that restored row in `outlabs_auth/services/auth.py:610–625`. This is supporting baseline evidence, not evidence of a future recovery implementation.
- **Counterexample:** a synthetic backup contains generation G0, runner epoch 4, committed watermark 100, source ownership enabled and an unrevoked session. Later epoch 5 applies watermark 200, the source is cut over and the session is revoked. A coordinated restore to the backup returns every ordinary database guard to the earlier state. A retained G0/4 plan at watermark 100 and the previously revoked session again agree with those guards. A normal commit-time check cannot know that a later authority boundary existed. Stopping old processes does not by itself reject a saved old plan submitted again after restart.
- **Consequence:** recovery can undo security withdrawal or source-authority cutover and admit stale membership/role contributions. The promise that recovery preserves isolation and stale-plan rejection is not established by a database-local monotonically increasing integer that can itself be restored.
- **Confidence and assumptions:** high confidence in the rollback counterexample; assumes epochs/cutover/revocation state are restored from the same backup without an independent boundary. The proposal does not claim a particular implementation. Its general requirement to specify a recovery runbook is already open; the new issue is the missing anti-rewind invariant that that runbook must satisfy.
- **Recommendation:** before any request, replay or outbox admission after restore, establish a new recovery generation that old credentials/plans/leases cannot name, and recover the accepted source-authority/high-water-mark state from evidence outside the restored failure domain. Alternatively, keep all affected mutation/authentication/replay admission closed until security state and cutover are explicitly reconstructed and accepted. Never silently downgrade an already accepted source cutover to the backup's coexistence policy. Include cache and pending delivery generations in the reconstruction policy.
- **Required proof:** restore a coordinated synthetic backup from before a source cutover, grant revocation and session rotation. Try an old runner, a saved old plan through a new runner, a revoked pre-restore refresh token, stale cache and a pending outbox item. All must be rejected or quarantined until explicit reconstruction; freshly admitted work under the new generation must succeed. Preserve checkpoint/history consistency and make the receipt identify the reconstructed authority boundary.
- **Gate affected / blocker classification:** **activation/deployment recovery blocker**, WP08/WP09 and G4/G5; revise the recovery guarantee before claiming its acceptance. Does not block additive schema work or the pure evaluator.
- **Suggested disposition:** revise; choose a bounded recovery admission contract, then test it in the recovery rehearsal.

### F-MR-02: “deduplicated delivery” lacks a contract for an uncertain external send

- **Severity:** Medium.
- **Evidence category:** proposal / interface acceptance gap; design counterexample.
- **Revision / lane:** revision 0.1 at the exact commit above; atomic replay and outbox.
- **Requirement / use case:** R20, R22, R24; U32, U36, U43.
- **Decision dependency:** D12/D13; observer versus outbox is already open, but external send ambiguity is not specified.
- **Claim and evidence:** `docs/TENANT_IDENTITIES_AND_ADMINISTRATIVE_GRANTS.md:1312–1318` promises identical committed-unit no-ops including delivery, followed by committed outbox records and “deduplicated delivery.” A12 at `1628` also requires deduplicated outbox behavior. The current mail interface accepts only a message (`outlabs_auth/mail/providers.py:40–42`); `AuthMailMessage` has no delivery-operation/idempotency contract (`outlabs_auth/mail/types.py:27–41`), and the result is accepted/failed rather than accepted/definitely rejected/unknown (`44–82`). SMTP sends and then returns an accepted result (`outlabs_auth/mail/providers.py:91–104`); a client exception cannot prove that external acceptance did not occur. A future outbox is not implemented by these interfaces.
- **Counterexample:** a committed invitation outbox row is pending. The worker sends; the transport accepts; the worker crashes before committing its receipt. Its local state is indistinguishable from a crash before transport acceptance. Retrying delivers twice in the first history; marking delivered or suppressing retry loses the invitation in the second. A unique outbox row prevents duplicate enqueueing but cannot distinguish these histories. A stable email Message-ID alone is not a receiver-side deduplication contract.
- **Consequence:** an implementation can satisfy atomic database checkpointing and still duplicate an invitation/notification or silently lose it. Calling delivery deduplicated without choosing the transport boundary overstates migration acceptance and complicates recovery.
- **Confidence and assumptions:** high confidence; assumes a supported transport without verified receiver/provider idempotency. No actual message was sent. If “deduplicated” means enqueue-only, that limitation needs to be explicit.
- **Recommendation:** distinguish duplicate-free enqueueing from external delivery. Specify either verified provider idempotency with a stable operation key and a documented retention window, or at-least-once delivery with an explicit unknown-outcome state, bounded retries and accepted duplicate risk. Retry the same immutable invitation/challenge payload; never rotate credentials to disguise a resend. Decide what an operator may safely retry after the provider's deduplication window expires.
- **Required proof:** inject failures before send, after external acceptance/before receipt, after receipt and after provider idempotency expiry. Check physical send counts under each declared transport policy, one logical invitation/token, no credential churn and honest receipts. Repeat the uncertain-send case during database recovery.
- **Gate affected / blocker classification:** **work-package blocker for the delivery contract**, WP06/G3, and activation of automatic replay delivery; not a blocker for namespace/topology approval. Supporting a non-idempotent provider is viable if the guarantee and owner acceptance are explicit.
- **Suggested disposition:** revise the guarantee and acceptance matrix; do not label outbox enqueue deduplication as proven duplicate-free delivery.

### F-MR-03: one logical source cut does not resolve contradictory lifecycle facts inside that cut

- **Severity:** Medium.
- **Evidence category:** host acceptance gap; design reasoning.
- **Revision / lane:** revision 0.1 at the exact commit above; removal facts and frozen history.
- **Requirement / use case:** R03, R20–R22; U12, U34, U42–U43.
- **Decision dependency:** D20/D22; source policy belongs to the host, but its minimum conflict/ordering contract is incomplete.
- **Claim and evidence:** `docs/TENANT_IDENTITIES_AND_ADMINISTRATIVE_GRANTS.md:1201–1209` preserves distinct removal/re-addition events, distinguishes removal time from observed absence and rejects changed payloads for the same event ID. Lines `1286–1291` quarantine conflicting export versions and establish one logical observation boundary. Lines `1293–1298` require a complete authoritative cohort before absence removal. These are necessary, but neither a common cut nor distinct event IDs resolves mutually inconsistent status facts for the same relationship/episode. There is no explicit requirement to declare collection precedence, lifecycle ordering or a conflict disposition when a present roster and a terminal removal record both claim current authority at that cut. Current membership state is one row per user/entity (`outlabs_auth/models/sql/entity_membership.py:65–68`), and the existing add path reactivates/replaces that state (`outlabs_auth/services/membership.py:244–274`); neither is a source lifecycle reducer.
- **Counterexample:** an atomic complete synthetic source export at watermark 300 contains relationship R in a roster declared current, while another authoritative record says the same relationship episode is terminally removed. The records have different valid event IDs and unchanged hashes. No later re-addition event or episode ID establishes which is authoritative. Treating presence as re-addition grants access; treating removal as final omits an accepted current member. Importing both as settled facts invents a chronology. This case is deliberately stronger than an ordinary old historical removal followed by an unambiguous current relationship.
- **Consequence:** all stated hash/completeness/snapshot checks can pass while replay fabricates a reactivation or a former-person event, or fails honest population accounting.
- **Confidence and assumptions:** medium-high confidence; requires contradictory source facts in the same logical cut. Some hosts can prove this impossible through their source constraints. Others must supply a deterministic, reviewed authority rule. This is not a request for Auth to embed private source semantics.
- **Recommendation:** require the host plan contract to identify authoritative current-state sources, relationship episode/incarnation rules and source event ordering or precedence. Contradictory same-cut facts must be a named conflict with no automatic access expansion and no invented removal/re-addition timestamp, actor or reason. Preserve raw observed facts and separately record the adopted disposition. If source constraints make contradiction impossible, include that proof in the completeness receipt.
- **Required proof:** complete same-cut active/removed contradiction; two differently keyed contradictory events; missing removal time; removed then explicitly re-added relationship; observed absence followed by reappearance; reused relationship IDs without proven episode identity. Assert exact accepted/conflicted counts, no fabricated historical facts and no unintended reactivation. Unambiguous source records must still migrate normally.
- **Gate affected / blocker classification:** **host activation/acceptance blocker for contradictory units**, G3/G5 and WP06/WP09. The library architecture can proceed with a fail-closed host conflict contract. This extends, rather than repeats, the existing consistent-snapshot gate.
- **Suggested disposition:** add the acceptance requirement and require owner disposition or quarantine for affected cohorts.

### F-MR-04: replay preservation needs an explicit exception or exclusion for login-identifier changes

- **Severity:** Medium.
- **Evidence category:** proposal cross-contract ambiguity.
- **Revision / lane:** revision 0.1 at the exact commit above; field ownership and credential compatibility.
- **Requirement / use case:** R07–R08, R15, R20; U22, U26, U43.
- **Decision dependency:** D19/D20; their intersection is not explicitly stated.
- **Claim and evidence:** `docs/TENANT_IDENTITIES_AND_ADMINISTRATIVE_GRANTS.md:1256–1260` permits a plan to declare each field source-owned/target-owned/shared, then says subsequent replay preserves verification and recovery/invitation state. Lines `1272–1273` permit source-owned business-field updates when their last-applied fingerprint agrees. In contrast, lines `730–735` require new-destination verification and invalidate old destination-bound state on identifier edits. The safe identifier rule is already explicit, but the replay contract does not say whether `User.email` or an authoritative phone-login identifier is excluded from ordinary source-owned fields, or whether its replay is a separately authorized security operation. Baseline `outlabs_auth/services/user.py:671–685,699–707` deliberately clears destination verification on email/phone changes; preserving a boolean across a replacement destination is not preserving its proof.
- **Counterexample:** the same stable source person ID changes its email. A plan declares login email source-owned; the target email still matches the last-applied fingerprint, so no local-edit conflict exists. Applying that field update while literally preserving verification/challenge/invitation state contradicts R15. Applying the supported identifier-change security behavior contradicts a literal blanket promise that ordinary replay leaves that state unchanged. UUID/crosswalk stability does not resolve this conflict.
- **Consequence:** implementers may silently treat an unverified replacement address as verified or preserve an old-destination challenge, or unexpectedly invalidate native flows during an ostensibly credential-preserving replay. The main security invariant rejects the unsafe interpretation; the report is not claiming that it authorizes such behavior.
- **Confidence and assumptions:** medium-high confidence in the wording conflict; assumes a host attempts to classify a login identifier as source-owned after provisioning. A host that makes all login identifiers target-owned avoids the case.
- **Recommendation:** explicitly make credential identifiers target-owned after initial provisioning by default, with source contact fields separate. Alternatively define source-driven identifier correction as a separately reviewed identifier-change operation with collision checks, epoch invalidation, new proof, delivery policy and audit, and document its exception to ordinary replay preservation. Source verification flags must never establish proof for a replacement destination by inheritance.
- **Required proof:** source email/phone change with unchanged target fingerprint, a local target identifier change, a pending old-destination challenge, verified/unverified replacements and same-email identities in another namespace. Ordinary replay must preserve the original native identity/flows or surface a correction request; the separately authorized change must invalidate exactly the destination-bound artifacts and leave unrelated-customer identities unchanged.
- **Gate affected / blocker classification:** **work-package contract clarification**, WP06/G3, before replay is allowed to alter login identifiers. May be safely excluded from initial replay scope. Not a newly discovered deficiency in the already-open all-flow D19 implementation work.
- **Suggested disposition:** revise the field-ownership contract with the explicit boundary and test.

## Recommended disposition

Keep the proposed namespace, legacy compatibility and migration work bounded behind their existing gates. Revise the recovery anti-rewind guarantee and the external delivery guarantee, then add the two replay ownership/lifecycle acceptance clarifications. The new findings do not justify redesigning customer namespaces or opening production work. A maintainer/human must still disposition the existing decisions; exact implementation and host acceptance evidence must then close each corresponding package and activation gate.
